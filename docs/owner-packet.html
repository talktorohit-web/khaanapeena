Part I

Complete Product Guide

A plain-language walkthrough of every screen and button — use it to run a demo and make the case for switching.

The pitch

One app runs the whole restaurant: billing, kitchen, tables, menu, inventory, purchasing, expenses, customers, 22 reports, cash register, staff, feedback and AI — on the hardware you already have, working even when the internet drops. Most competitors sell these as separate paid modules; here they're one bundle.

A dinner service, end-to-end
  1. Guests sit at table 4 → Tables → tap T4, pick the waiter looking after them and tap 👥 for how many are seated (or let them scan the table QR and order from their own phone).
  2. In Billing, tap dishes or use 🎙️ voice. Dishes with choices ask first — half or full, spice level, extra cheese — and a 📝 on any line sends a cooking note to the kitchen.
  3. Press 🔥 Send KOT (F4). The Kitchen screen shows the ticket instantly with the choices in bold; stock auto-deducts by recipe.
  4. Cook marks ✓ Ready then ✓ Served; late tickets glow red.
  5. At payment, 💳 Settle (F9) → UPI / Cash / Card / Udhaar, or split it half cash half UPI. Waive the service charge if the guest declines, add a phone for loyalty, add a firm's GSTIN if they need one → ✓ Collect & Close Bill. A GST-correct invoice prints.
  6. Reports, food cost, expenses, the cash drawer and AI Insights all update themselves; the owner runs a Z-report at close and reads the 🌙 Day-end master page.

How a bill adds up

The order matters — this is what prints, top to bottom
  1. Sub-total — every dish, including any priced add-ons.
  2. Less discount — ₹ or %, if one was given.
  3. Service charge 5% (optional) — on the discounted value. It prints with the word “optional” on it, because a service charge is voluntary in India and the guest is entitled to see that on the slip. Removed entirely if they decline.
  4. CGST + SGST — charged on food plus service charge, because a service charge is part of the value of supply and is taxable. Taxing only the food would under-declare the bill.
  5. Total, rounded to the rupee.

₹1,000 of food → ₹50 service charge → GST on ₹1,050 → ₹1,103. On the Composition scheme steps 4 is skipped entirely and the bill prints as a Bill of Supply.

The slip closes with your name and your phone — “THANKS! Visit again / Powered by «your restaurant» / Contact: «your number»”. The guest never sees our name or our support line; it is your bill, not our advertisement. Set both in Settings → Restaurant details, and if you leave the phone blank the Contact line simply doesn't print.

✓
Why it matters: the whole loop is 3 key-presses, works offline, never duplicates an invoice number, and the UPI QR collects digital payments with zero extra hardware or gateway fees. Every way money can leave — a discount, a freebie, a write-off, a cancelled dish — now has to say who and why before the bill will close.

Before any report is chosen, the top of the page answers the two questions an owner actually walks in asking: what have I taken so far, and what is still sitting on the tables. 🛡️ Who did what — without slowing anyone down

Every bill, discount, void and refund carries a person's name, so the staff reports and employee-of-the-month actually mean something.

The simple way (how it ships, no PINs). Open the Cash Register each morning and pick who's on the till — every bill that shift is credited to them. If someone takes over, switch the On the till picker in the sidebar: one tap, no PIN, nothing to remember. Assign a waiter as each table is seated and dine-in gets attributed more precisely still. Nobody is locked out of anything.

The strict way (optional). Only if you also want to restrict what each role can see — a cashier never seeing your margins, only a manager opening the drawer — turn on Staff roles & permissions in Settings. Then each person unlocks the till with their own PIN and sees only their screens; you use the Manager PIN for full access. Sensible defaults are built in (a Waiter sees Billing/Tables/Kitchen; a Cook sees only the kitchen) and all of it is customisable. Settings checks every PIN before you switch it on and warns about clashes.

Either way, the Manager PIN stays the gate for the things that cost money: editing a dish already sent to the kitchen, approving a discount, writing a bill off as not chargeable, and refunding a settled bill.

30-minute smoke test, then reset

  1. Ring up ~10 bills end-to-end — order → KOT → settle → print.
  2. Do one split bill, one table transfer and one move items between tables.
  3. Settle one bill split half cash half UPI, and one on udhaar — then collect it in Reports → Udhaar.
  4. Do a void with the Manager PIN — confirm stock returns and the reason is asked.
  5. Give one discount as ₹ and one as % — confirm the box is locked and empty until the manager PIN is entered, that the total moves, and that neither closes without a reason.
  6. Check one bill's service charge and GST — ₹1,000 of food should read ₹50 service charge, ₹52.50 GST, ₹1,103 total, and the line must print the word (optional).
  7. Open & close a cash shift — pick who's on the till; check the Z-report tallies after booking a cash expense.
  8. Place one QR self-order — confirm it reaches the kitchen with its choices and notes.
  9. Send one bill to a remote payer — confirm the WhatsApp opens with the amount, then settle and send the receipt.
  10. Open Reports → 🌙 Day-end, then export one report as 📄 PDF — that's your nightly routine.
  11. Reset demo data before real service.
